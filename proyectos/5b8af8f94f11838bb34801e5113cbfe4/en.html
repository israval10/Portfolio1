Imagine being an insurer's client, relying on it to protect what you best value, as your health or your home. At the time of an incident that requires a claim, what should be a simple process quickly becomes a series of frustrations: long waiting on the phone line, endless transfers between agents with no context of your case and the feeling of being lost in a process where they appear to speak another language.

The Current Challenge

That's how this company looks. Your customers face challenges due to inefficient monitoring of their claims, preventing them from monitoring their status and creating a sense of uncertainty. The care process is opaque and lacks clarity, and this directly affects the client's satisfaction and thus his reputation.

The minimum Viable Product (PMV) to be developed aims at providing users with a tool to track their claims in real time, enabling them to manage their case monitoring without constant direct contact with their personnel. The benefits at the corporate level translate into an optimization of resources and an acceleration of the process.

The Expected Impact

It aims at improving its operational efficiency and strengthening its relationship with its customers. By enabling them to manage themselves, an insurer can generate a more fluent experience, increasing customers' confidence and ultimately improving their satisfaction and loyalty.

LEAN

Use of 4 lean principles in the organization

The Just In Time (JIT) system aims at ensuring that services are delivered at the right time and place and eliminating any waste that does not generate value. In the context of an insurance company's claims platform, applying the Lean methodology aims at improving communication with customers, ensuring efficiency in their demand management and increasing satisfaction by providing precise and timely information about their claims status. The following describes how the Lean Principles are implemented in this system:

Flow

The flow principle ensures that demand monitoring activities are continuous and continuous. Currently, the identified bottle necks include long times to update claims status and lack of automated notifications for customers. To improve the flow we propose:

  • Digitize the whole demand monitoring process using a track system that allows real-time status viewing.
  • Implement automatic alerts by push notifications at every step change of the claim.
  • Automate repetitive tasks, such as agent assignment for more complex cases, using predictive systems.

These measures seek to reduce dead times, increase process transparency and minimize uncertainty among customers.

Pull

The Pull principle ensures that resources are used only with a real demand. In the case of the claims platform, this means activating the actions of the system only when the client submits a query or when the claim changes status. To implement this principle:

  • To develop an interface that will allow customers to consult their claim status at any time without contacting their staff.
  • To integrate prioritization tools based on case urgency or time from start
  • Replacing manual monitoring with a dynamic system that optimizes assignment of tasks to staff as currently charged

That will reduce assignment of tasks, optimize agent time and improve client experience.

Rhythm

The rhythm or "takt time" defines the predictable cadence for updates and closure of claims. To set this rate means balancing operational capacity with customers expectations. To define an appropriate rhythm:

  • Analyze historic data about mean demand resolution times to identify high and low demand patterns.
  • To set standard deadlines for each stage of the process, as the maximum time allowed to answer inquiries or solve simple cases.
  • Synchronize system tasks with agent activities, ensuring that notifications reach customers in real time.

These actions will contribute to improving service predictability, increasing customer satisfaction and optimizing operational burden.

Zero defects

The Zero Flaw principle aims at preventing errors at every stage of the claims process, which is crucial to maintaining customer confidence in the insurance company. The following actions are proposed:

  • Implement automatic validation on the platform to avoid data entry errors or assignment of tasks.
  • To design a centralized system that keeps a single and up-to-date registry of each claim, eliminating redundancy and guaranteeing precise information.
  • Training care personnel in using tracking and effective communication technologies.

The measures are intended to significantly reduce administrative errors, improve information accuracy and strengthen customer confidence in the company

Kanban board

To illustrate and contextualize the use of the tool in the project, a Kanban board representation has been created to reflect the status of work performed by the team during the second week of the Sprint.

The Kanban board used for the project is structured into five fundamental phases that secure an organized and efficient workflow. The "Backlog" column includes all tasks to be performed at today's Sprint. The "To Do" column houses the tasks selected to start at once, with an emphasis on top priority and avoiding overload. The jobs are later advanced to "In Progress," where work that's currently under development. Once completed, they move to the To Verify column, where they are undergoing review and verification processes to ensure quality, alignment with requirements and compliance with the Fact Definition (DOD). Finally, while passing to the Die column, this indicates that they have been finalized and ready to be delivered.
